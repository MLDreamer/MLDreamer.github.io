About

I don't believe in AI magic.

I believe in the mathematics. That isn't a tagline — it's a filter. It tells me which problems are worth solving, which architectures will survive production, and which AI initiatives are theatre dressed as transformation.

How I got here

I came up through mathematics. A Boeing-sponsored PhD from IIT Bombay in Optimization & Operations Research — stochastic programming, large-scale combinatorial optimization, simulation under uncertainty — gave me something rare in this field: the ability to prove what I build, not just demo it. Before that, an M.Tech from NIT Tiruchirappalli and a B.Tech in Mechanical Engineering.

Then quantitative finance (WorldQuant, a Geneva family office), where feedback is instant and brutal — a wrong model costs money the same day. It installed an evaluation discipline I never lost. Then production ML for a US credit card company, healthcare NLP for CVS Health, and nearly four years at Unilever.

At Unilever I built the forecasting and decision-intelligence function from scratch — no inherited team, no existing infrastructure. A Temporal Fusion Transformer zero-shot forecasting platform across 30+ new markets under data sparsity, 25,000+ SKUs, 200+ planners daily, 90+ markets. Full stack: feature store, MLflow, real-time inference, drift monitoring, SAP IBP. Hired and led a 30-person org; governed EY, Deloitte and BCG concurrently. The numbers — 4–5pp accuracy step-change, £2.5M+ revenue uplift, 20% fewer stockouts — came from the mathematics, not the marketing.

Now at Cognizant I work on the harder problem: what comes after the model. I architect enterprise agentic AI platforms — autonomous agent fleets that are observable, self-healing and governed — including semantic ontologies that let Google Gemini agents reason across heterogeneous enterprise data, with multi-cloud orchestration across AWS Bedrock, GCP Vertex AI and Azure OpenAI.

What I care about

Most teams building agents are solving for capability. I'm solving for industrialisation. Trust and evaluation are the product, not an afterthought. The hard part is not the model — it's proving, to a regulator and a skeptic, when a system is right, when it is unsure, and when it should stop and ask a human.

I am wary of fluent chain-of-thought that only reads like reasoning. I design for the moment a model is confidently wrong, because that's the expensive failure: deterministic guardrails, human-in-the-loop escalation, audit trails, and evals that reflect the real distribution — not a curated demo.
